## What are the goals of our team?: Our Goals Statement

Our team wants to learn how to write code more efficiently, learn new languages, and complete a full-stack development process by working together. One goal in specific is to produce a polished, functional, and complete website to optimize the user experience. One skill we want to develop is efficient collaboration: getting things done quickly by dividing efforts while avoiding duplicating our efforts.

## How will we capitalize on the strengths of each member?

First, we are using Trello to organize tasks and responsibilites, which will help us take advantage of our strengths.

Our plan is to be flexible, inspired by the **Agile** philosophy. We aren't going to predetermine our roles, and we're going to fall into whatever feels natural to us, while emphasizing communication and mutual respect to smoothen the workflow.

## What are the expectations that will guide our team?

### When will your team meet? What time, how often, for how long, where?

Our current plan is to meet every Sunday, at 12 in the library. We will work for as long as necessary or as long as it takes for us to understand our tasks and then work asynchronously.

### What roles will members take on in your meetings? How often will you rotate roles? Who is responsible for setting agendas, taking notes, facilitating discussions, etc?

We will rotate based on what works best for people at the time, making sure that the agenda is clear and sufficient notes are taken.

### How will you communicate with each other? (to share work, to ask questions, notify the group if someone is running late or if someone will miss a meeting, etc)

Our current communication stack is Trello, text, CampusWire, GitHub, and email.

### How will you make sure communication stays respectful? (How does your team define “respectful”?)

We define respectful as keeping everyone's feelings and thoughts in mind when making decisions, and communicating with the group. We will enforce this by boosting team morale through friendly communication.

### What are the expectations for dealing with a teammate who hasn’t been communicating? How frequently should team members communicate / check in?

We will use text to make sure everyone is on board. Text allows for fast and effective communication, so team members will check in as frequently as it seems necessary.

### What technologies will you use to support team meetings and work? (Google Drive, Hangouts, Zoom, Facetime, etc)

We are using Trello, a sort of Kanban board to support team work, along with text and email to schedule in person meetings.

### How will you make decisions? (Unanimous, consensus, majority rule, by assigned roles, rock-paper-scissors, etc.) How will you address conflict or deal with disagreements within the team?

We believe in the art of compromise. Everyone respects each other and we believe we can deal with conflicts of interest through seeking the best solution for everyone.

### How will you divide the work?

Through in person meetings, the work can be divided in real time as we check in on each other and communicate face to face.

### How will you ensure that everybody participates meaningfully? How will you make sure that everyone’s contribution is valued?

We are all eager to contribute, so worrying about people not participating meaninfully isn't our top concern. Everyone's contribution will be helpful because we won't duplicate efforts.

### What expectations do you have for satisfactory participation? (How much time will each group member spend per week on project activities?)

If we assign tasks such that everyone has a roughly equal time commitment, and help each other when needed, we can make sure no one is swamped by one aspect of the project.

### What process will you follow if someone does not live up to their responsibilities and/or meet the standards for work set by the team?

We will send people a gentle reminder, and if that doesn't work, we will have a more formal discussion with Jean. We don't think this will be a problem, though.
